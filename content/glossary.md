## Season-relative qualification

Rate-stat leaderboards need a minimum amount of playing time, or a hot week tops every list. Instead of one flat floor, the service derives the threshold from the schedule each player's teams actually faced:

- **Batters:** 3.1 plate appearances per team game (the modern MLB rule).
- **Pitchers:** one inning per team game.

A 154-game 1927 season, a 162-game 1998 season and a 74-game 1943 Negro National League season each get their own bar.

Career qualification adds up the season thresholds a player's teams faced, instead of using a flat 3,000 at-bats. That is why Josh Gibson's documented plate appearances qualify against the 60–80 game schedules he actually played; a flat floor would erase him. A 100-PA minimum also guards against seasons where only a handful of games were documented.

Choosing **Qualified** in a leaderboard's minimum filter applies this rule. Choosing a fixed minimum (for example *500 AB*) replaces it.

## Columns

| Column | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| AVG | Batting average: hits per at-bat. |
| OBP | On-base percentage. |
| SLG | Slugging percentage: total bases per at-bat. |
| OPS | On-base plus slugging. |
| ERA | Earned runs allowed per nine innings. |
| WHIP | Walks plus hits per inning pitched. |
| K/9, BB/9 | Strikeouts and walks per nine innings pitched. |
| FPct | Fielding percentage: (PO + A) / (PO + A + E). |
| IP | Innings pitched, in baseball notation: .1 and .2 are one and two thirds of an inning. |

## Where the numbers stop short

- **No park factors.** Lahman has no home/road run splits, so no rate here is park-adjusted.
- **Negro Leagues team games reflect documented play.** Where the record is thin, schedule-relative thresholds are correspondingly lenient. See *Surviving Records*.
- **Early-era gaps are honest gaps.** Sacrifice flies before 1954 and hit-by-pitch before 1887 weren't recorded, so early OBP is approximate. Missing values show a dash instead of an invented number.
- For the general limits of the data, see the data scope statement on the *About* page.
